## 📊 Project Overview

This project performs Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) on customer banking data to identify factors associated with personal loan acceptance.

The analysis explores customer demographics, income, education, family size, banking behavior, credit card usage, online banking usage, and mortgage values.

An interactive Excel dashboard was created using PivotTables, PivotCharts, and slicers to present the key findings.

---

## 🎯 Business Objective

The main objective of this project is to understand:

- Which customer characteristics are associated with personal loan acceptance?
- Does income category influence loan acceptance?
- Does age range influence loan acceptance?
- Does education level show differences in loan acceptance?
- Does family size show any clear relationship with loan acceptance?
- Is online banking usage different between loan and non-loan customers?
- Do customers with higher mortgage values show higher personal loan acceptance?

## 💡 Key Insights

### 1. Income shows a strong association with personal loan acceptance

Customers in the **100+ income category** have a substantially higher personal loan acceptance rate compared with lower-income categories.

- 0–50 income: **0%**
- 51–100 income: approximately **2.3%**
- 100+ income: approximately **36.1%**

This indicates that income is one of the strongest variables associated with personal loan acceptance in this dataset.

### 2. Age shows relatively little difference

Personal loan acceptance rates are relatively similar across the age groups:

- Middle: approximately **9.25%**
- Senior: approximately **9.30%**
- Young: approximately **10.53%**

Therefore, age appears to have a weaker association with loan acceptance than income.

### 3. Online banking usage is almost similar

Approximately **60.46%** of personal-loan customers use online banking compared with approximately **59.50%** of non-loan customers.

This indicates only a small difference in online banking usage between the two groups.

### 4. Personal-loan customers have higher average mortgage values

Average mortgage value:

| Personal Loan | Average Mortgage |
|---|---:|
| No | 51.80 |
| Yes | 101.27 |

Personal-loan customers have a higher average mortgage value than non-loan customers.

### 5. Income differences are visible across education groups

Personal-loan customers have higher average income across all education levels.

| Education | Loan = No | Loan = Yes |
|---|---:|---:|
| Graduate | 52.28 | 144.98 |
| Professional | 53.16 | 148.04 |
| Undergraduate | 83.14 | 136.88 |

Professional personal-loan customers have the highest average income among the three education groups.

### 6. Family size shows no clear pattern

Personal loan acceptance is observed across different family-size groups, with no strong or consistent pattern based solely on family size.

---

## 📊 Key Business Finding

Overall, the analysis suggests that **financial characteristics, particularly income and mortgage value, show stronger associations with personal loan acceptance than demographic variables such as age and family size.**

This insight could help financial institutions identify customer segments that may be more relevant for targeted personal-loan campaigns.
